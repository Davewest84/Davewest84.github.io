The Mandelson affair is the only show in town politically this morning. Keir Starmer is facing calls to resign after it emerged that Lord Mandelson failed security vetting before being appointed UK ambassador to Washington. UK Security Vetting, the Cabinet Office division responsible for scrutinising senior appointments, did not grant clearance, but the Foreign Office intervened and overturned the recommendation. Kemi Badenoch has accused the Prime Minister of misleading Parliament, pointing to his three separate assurances at the dispatch box last September that "full due process" was followed. Starmer has apologised for the appointment, accusing Mandelson of a "litany of deceit" about his ties to Jeffrey Epstein, and has promised to release the relevant documents. The story has gone international, with CNN among those picking it up. It is the most serious political crisis of his premiership so far, and it is clearly not going away.

There are several NHS and health angles worth flagging this morning. That Covid inquiry finding on vaccine uptake inequalities is significant, particularly the gaps by deprivation and ethnicity, which fits squarely in the health inequalities space. There is also a scientific dispute over the Alzheimer's drugs lecanemab and donanemab. A new review claims neither provides meaningful clinical benefit to patients, though other researchers have pushed back, defending their modest effectiveness. These drugs are on the NICE assessment pathway, so this controversy could well become an NHS commissioning story. Separately, the NHS waiting list for routine hospital treatment in England has fallen to seven point two two million, the lowest level in three years. That is a useful number for the government, but it still represents an enormous backlog.
