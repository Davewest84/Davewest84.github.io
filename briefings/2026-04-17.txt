The biggest story in Westminster is the Mandelson vetting crisis, and it is a genuine crisis for Starmer. It emerged yesterday, through the Guardian, that Lord Mandelson failed his initial security vetting from UK Security Vetting before being appointed US ambassador, but the Foreign Office used a rarely invoked authority to override the recommendation and clear him for the post. That matters because Starmer told Parliament three times last September that "full due process" was followed. Recall that Starmer later sacked Mandelson after a US Congressional committee revealed new details about his ties to Jeffrey Epstein, so this is another layer on an already damaging saga. Opposition leaders spent the day calling for Starmer to resign. Kemi Badenoch accused him of misleading the House. Ed Davey called it a "catastrophic error of judgment" and accused the prime minister of lying to the British public. The government's line is that Starmer was unaware the vetting had been overruled until this week, and he has ordered the Foreign Office to explain.
