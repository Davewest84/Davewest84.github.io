The Falklands are back in the headlines, and it's because of Donald Trump. A leaked Pentagon email proposed reviewing US backing for Britain's sovereignty over the islands as punishment for the UK not supporting the war in Iran. The memo suggested reducing what it called Europe's "sense of entitlement" and explicitly named the Falklands as leverage. Starmer responded quickly, reaffirming that sovereignty rests with the UK and that the islanders' right to self-determination is not up for debate. Yvette Cooper and Kemi Badenoch both echoed that, making it a rare moment of genuine cross-party unity. Argentina has predictably renewed its call for talks. This is the sharpest escalation yet in the UK-US relationship since the Iran crisis began, and it will be interesting to see whether it stays as posturing or becomes actual policy.

The other major story from yesterday is the assisted dying bill, which is now dead. It ran out of parliamentary time in the House of Lords after opponents tabled more than 1,200 amendments, effectively filibustering it into the ground. The bill had passed two votes in the Commons but never came to a vote in the Lords. Charlie Falconer, who sponsored it in the upper house, called it "pure obstructionism." Kim Leadbeater, who introduced it in the Commons, says she'll try again next session by entering the private members' ballot. Dame Esther Rantzen's daughter described herself as furious. For a bill that had broader parliamentary support than any previous attempt at legalising assisted dying in England and Wales, it is a genuinely deflating outcome.

And the Starmer leadership question continues to build. That has moved beyond backbench muttering into cabinet-level territory. The question is whether the election results, combined with the Mandelson scandal, create enough momentum for a serious challenge.
