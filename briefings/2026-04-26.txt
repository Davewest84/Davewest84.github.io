On health, the hospice funding crisis is worth watching this week. The Public Accounts Committee has warned that the government's response to the financial collapse in the hospice sector lacks urgency. Nearly 60 per cent of English hospices have made or are considering cuts to frontline services, and around 380 beds have been shut because hospices simply cannot afford to staff them. Integrated care boards provide on average only about a third of hospice funding, with the rest coming from charitable donations. The government has announced a strategic commissioning development programme launching this month, but the modern service framework it depends on won't be complete until autumn, and the PAC is clear the pace doesn't match the crisis. Separately, A&E waiting times fell to a five-year low in March despite the NHS seeing a record 2.43 million attendances, which is a genuinely positive data point worth noting.
