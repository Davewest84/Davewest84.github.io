The election results are in, and they amount to the most significant realignment in British politics in a generation. Reform UK has gained more than 1,400 council seats across England, flipping at least twelve councils including Sunderland, Wigan and Newcastle-under-Lyme. In Wigan, a former mining community Labour controlled for more than fifty years, the party lost every single one of its twenty seats to Reform. Across England, Labour has lost nearly a thousand councillors and thirty councils. The Conservatives also took a beating, losing 223 seats, mostly to Reform. The Liberal Democrats gained 28 seats and the Greens gained 50, taking their total to 90. Keir Starmer held a press conference yesterday insisting he will not resign, but pressure is building fast. The Transport Salaried Staffs Association has publicly called for him to go, and reports suggest several senior figures including Wes Streeting and Andy Burnham are positioning themselves for a possible leadership challenge. Nigel Farage declared it a "truly historic shift in British politics."

In Wales, the result was even more dramatic. Labour dropped to just nine Senedd seats, and First Minister Eluned Morgan lost her own constituency, making her the first head of government in UK history to lose their seat while in office. Plaid Cymru won 43 seats and will now look to form a government, while Reform came second with 34. In Scotland, the SNP held on as the largest party but fell short of a majority, and the Greens took two notable seats, unseating Angus Robertson in Edinburgh Central and taking Nicola Sturgeon's former Glasgow Southside constituency.
