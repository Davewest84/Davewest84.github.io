Yesterday's State Opening of Parliament went ahead against this extraordinary backdrop. The King's Speech contained 37 bills, and the one that matters most for your world is the NHS Modernisation Bill. This will formally abolish NHS England and transfer its powers back to the Department of Health and Social Care. It also moves pharmacy commissioning to integrated care boards and commits to a single patient record available through the NHS App, starting with maternity and frailty care by 2028. Healthwatch will also be abolished under the plans. The King's Fund responded cautiously, warning the bill risks pulling power back to the centre while disbanding the independent bodies set up to listen to patients. For HSJ readers, this is the legal underpinning for everything the 10 Year Health Plan set out, and it's going to dominate the policy landscape for months to come.

There's an obvious collision between these two stories. If Streeting does resign today, the new health secretary will inherit the Modernisation Bill and the entire reform agenda at a critical moment. Whether Streeting wins a leadership contest or not, his departure from the health brief would reshape the politics of NHS reform completely.
