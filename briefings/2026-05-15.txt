Good morning Dave, here's your briefing for Friday the 15th of May 2026.

The big story, and the one that directly affects everything you do, is that Wes Streeting resigned as health secretary yesterday. His letter to Starmer stopped just short of formally triggering a leadership contest, but left absolutely no doubt about where he's heading. He wrote that he had "lost confidence" in Starmer's leadership and said it would be "dishonourable and unprincipled" to remain in government. Where the country needs vision, he wrote, there is "a vacuum," and where it needs direction, there is "drift." He did claim credit on health on his way out, noting that waiting lists fell by a hundred and ten thousand in March, the biggest monthly drop outside Covid since 2008. He'll need the backing of 81 Labour MPs to trigger a formal contest, and with 92 already publicly calling for Starmer to go, that threshold looks very much within reach.

James Murray, previously chief secretary to the treasury, was appointed as Streeting's replacement within hours. And then later in the evening, Andy Burnham confirmed he plans to return to Westminster. Makerfield MP Josh Simons has agreed to stand down to clear a path for the Greater Manchester mayor to win a parliamentary seat and mount his own leadership challenge. Burnham hinted that any formal contest should wait until he's actually in the Commons, asking for "space and stability" while the by-election takes place. But the direction of travel is unmistakable. Starmer is refusing to budge, though the field of challengers keeps growing. Angela Rayner is also widely seen as positioning herself, so this is now a three-way contest at minimum.

For HSJ and for every NHS leader waking up this morning, the Murray appointment is the immediate question. He has no health background, coming from the Treasury and before that housing policy under Sadiq Khan. He now inherits the NHS Modernisation Bill, which was introduced to Parliament literally the day before he was appointed, and which formally abolishes NHS England, restructures integrated care boards, folds the Health Services Safety Investigations Body into the CQC, and abolishes Healthwatch. He picks up the entire 10 Year Health Plan at its most critical legislative moment. The question across the system today will be whether Murray simply carries on with Streeting's programme or looks to put his own stamp on the brief, and what either path means for the reform timetable.
