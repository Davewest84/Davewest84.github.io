Good morning Dave, here's your briefing for Saturday the 16th of May 2026.

The Labour leadership crisis has entered a decisive new phase. Andy Burnham was approved by Labour's National Executive Committee yesterday to stand for selection in the Makerfield by-election, after backbencher Josh Simons resigned his seat specifically to clear the way. This is a significant shift from February, when the NEC blocked Burnham from standing in the Gorton and Denton by-election. A Survation poll of Labour members published yesterday found 61 per cent would back Burnham over Starmer in a head-to-head contest, with the Prime Minister on just 28 per cent. Wes Streeting, who resigned as health secretary on Wednesday, has publicly backed Burnham as Labour's strongest candidate. The by-election is expected on the 18th of June, and if Burnham wins the seat, a formal leadership challenge could follow rapidly. Burnham's allies are briefing that he could be in Downing Street by autumn.

The other big domestic story is a meningitis outbreak in Reading that has killed one student and left two others in hospital. The teenager who died attended The Henley College in Oxfordshire, while the other confirmed cases are linked to Reading Blue Coat School and Highdown School. One case has been confirmed as meningitis B, though UK Health Security Agency says it is not the same strain responsible for the Kent outbreak earlier this year. Close contacts are being offered antibiotics as a precaution.

The change of health secretary is obviously a huge story for the NHS and for HSJ. James Murray, previously chief secretary to the treasury, replaced Streeting within hours of his resignation on Thursday. Murray brings a strong understanding of public spending, and his most recent public remarks on health came at an Institute for Government event on public service productivity, which you were quick to transcribe and send to Alastair. The question for the NHS now is whether Murray will maintain Streeting's reform agenda, particularly on the 10 Year Health Plan and the Provider Improvement Programme, or whether the political turmoil means health policy effectively goes into a holding pattern. Separately, the government announced yesterday that a single patient record system could save the NHS 110 million pounds. That commitment sits within the NHS Modernisation Bill introduced in Wednesday's King's Speech, which will formally abolish NHS England and transfer its functions to the Department of Health and Social Care. And from HSJ's own newsletter yesterday, the Southport attack story has developed an NHS dimension, with a victim accusing a trust of a cover-up after hospital staff inappropriately accessed their medical records.
