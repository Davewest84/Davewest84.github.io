On the NHS front, the Financial Times is running with the Palantir patient data story. NHS England has confirmed that external staff from Palantir and other consultancy firms have been given a new admin role granting broader access to identifiable patient data on the National Data Integration Tenant, the platform that is supposed to pseudonymise records before they are shared. The change was made because applying for individual controlled data access permissions had become, in NHS England's words, too inconvenient. Privacy campaigners and clinicians have raised concerns about the scale of access, and Palantir's wider work with US defence and immigration agencies adds to the political sensitivity. Worth flagging to the team as a data governance story with legs.

Internationally, three big stories. The World Health Organisation has declared the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda a public health emergency of international concern. It involves the Bundibugyo strain, which has no approved vaccine or treatment. As of Saturday there were eight lab-confirmed cases but 246 suspected cases and 80 suspected deaths in Ituri Province, with cases now confirmed in Kinshasa and across the border in Uganda. The WHO says the outbreak is likely larger than currently detected.

Ukraine launched one of its largest ever drone strikes on Russia overnight on Saturday, sending around 500 drones at oil infrastructure. At least four people were killed near Moscow and a dozen wounded, with the Moscow oil refinery among the targets. Zelensky said Ukraine's long-range capabilities are significantly changing the situation on the ground.

On the Trump-Iran nuclear talks, the US proposed a 20-year suspension of uranium enrichment on Friday, a softening from Trump's earlier demand for a permanent halt. But Ayatollah Khamenei rejected it over the weekend, calling the demand excessive and accusing Trump of speaking of olive branches while showing barbed wire.
