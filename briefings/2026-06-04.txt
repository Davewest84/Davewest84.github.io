The Henry Nowak case has entered a more dangerous phase. Nigel Farage warned yesterday that the rioting in Southampton risks being "the beginning" of wider unrest, accusing the government of operating two-tier policing. Keir Starmer hit back directly, calling Farage out for exploiting the tragedy for political gain and denying the two-tier policing claim. During Tuesday's protests, eleven police officers and a police dog were injured as crowds threw flares, bricks, bins and an e-scooter at riot lines, forcing officers to retreat in some areas. Nowak's father has appealed for calm, insisting this is a case about murder, not about race or religion. Hampshire Police is under sustained pressure. The force has already apologised to the family, and the IOPC is investigating, but with more protests expected across the country, this story is only escalating.

The Mandelson affair is still producing fresh embarrassment for Downing Street. Undisclosed text messages have emerged showing Pat McFadden, Starmer's chief secretary to the Cabinet Office, consoled Lord Mandelson after his dismissal as US ambassador last September. The 1,500 pages of vetting documents released this week add to the drip of revelations at the worst possible time, with somewhere around 80 to 95 Labour MPs now calling on Starmer to name a departure date.

The second RMT tube strike hits today. London Underground drivers walk out from midnight for 24 hours in the continuing dispute over TfL's compressed four-day week proposals. ASLEF drivers are not joining the action, so most lines will run on reduced service, but TfL warns there will be no service at all on the Circle, Piccadilly, parts of the Metropolitan and parts of the Central line. Bear it in mind for tonight.

On health, the Kent meningitis outbreak remains under close watch. Health officials said yesterday it is too early to say whether the outbreak has peaked or whether a national vaccination programme should be recommended. One to keep an eye on if it develops further.

Internationally, the US House of Representatives has passed a war powers resolution directing President Trump to end hostilities with Iran. The vote was 215 to 208, with four Republicans breaking ranks. It is largely symbolic since the Senate is unlikely to pass it and Trump would certainly veto, but it marks the first time either chamber has cleared such a measure since the conflict began more than 90 days ago. The frustration on Capitol Hill is growing, with some Republicans openly questioning the absence of a clear endgame.

A devastating hotel fire in New Delhi killed at least 21 people yesterday, 18 of them foreign nationals. The blaze began in a ground-floor restaurant and raced through the upper floors of the Flourish Inn Stay hotel in the Malviya Nagar district while guests were still asleep. Videos showed people jumping from windows to escape the smoke. A preliminary investigation found the hotel had no valid fire safety certificate. Prime Minister Modi has expressed condolences and the city authorities have ordered a full inquiry.

Russia launched another massive aerial assault on Ukraine this week, with more than 650 drones and missiles striking cities including Kyiv and Dnipro. In response, Ukrainian drones hit an oil terminal in St Petersburg, setting it ablaze during Putin's annual economic forum, a pointed piece of timing.
