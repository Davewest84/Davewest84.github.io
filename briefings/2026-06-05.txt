The Nowak case has opened a new front. Keir Starmer hit out at Elon Musk yesterday, accusing him of "trying to whip up division" over the murder of Henry Nowak. Andy Burnham has made his move. In a BBC Question Time special, the Greater Manchester mayor confirmed he would enter a Labour leadership contest if one opens up. He said he thinks Wes Streeting seems to have launched a leadership contest, so if that is running, he would seek to join it. He acknowledged he first needs to win the Makerfield by-election on the 18th of June after Josh Simons stepped down. The betting markets already have Burnham as favourite to replace Starmer, and with 80 to 95 Labour MPs calling for a departure date, the pressure on the prime minister shows no sign of easing.

The BBC is preparing for its biggest round of cuts in 15 years. Up to 2,000 jobs, roughly ten per cent of the workforce, will go over the next two years. Interim director general Rhodri Talfan Davies is expected to announce the redundancies, with non-content roles in HR, finance, legal and operations bearing the initial brunt under an internal programme called Project Ada. The corporation is targeting around 100 million pounds in savings from back-office functions alone, driven by falling licence fee revenue and pressure from streaming competitors. A voluntary redundancy scheme will be opened to try to avoid compulsory layoffs, but staff face an anxious wait until September for the full detail.

Internationally, the biggest development is a reported preliminary agreement between the US and Iran. The two sides have reportedly reached a memorandum of understanding to extend the ceasefire for a further 60 days and begin negotiations to permanently end the war. Trump said talks are continuing but cautioned the outcome remains unclear. This comes after days of escalation, with US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island and Iranian missiles fired at American forces in the region. If the ceasefire extension holds, it would be the most significant step towards de-escalation since the conflict began on the 28th of February.

The US House of Representatives passed an eight billion dollar Ukraine aid package on Wednesday, with 18 Republicans breaking ranks to defy Trump and GOP leadership. The bill includes loans to Ukraine and NATO allies, plus more than a billion in other funds for Baltic security and Radio Free Europe, and imposes new oil and gas sanctions on Russia. The White House says Trump will veto it, and Senate Republican leaders have not allowed votes on Russia sanctions legislation, so its path to becoming law is narrow, but the bipartisan rebellion is notable.

And John Bolton, the former national security adviser, is set to plead guilty to retaining classified information, according to multiple US reports. Bolton served under Trump during his first term and has been a vocal critic since.
