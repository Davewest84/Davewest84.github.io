The Nowak story has escalated into a full diplomatic row with Washington. On defence, the chief of the defence staff Richard Knighton warned on Friday that the UK is running out of time to strengthen its defences against Russia. He told the BBC that Russia is "definitely raising the stakes" through more incursions into British airspace, cyberattacks, sabotage, and attempts to steal technology. Internationally, the Israel-Lebanon ceasefire agreed last Tuesday is already in serious trouble. Israeli airstrikes on Saturday killed nine people in southern Lebanon, including three Lebanese army officers. Hezbollah has formally rejected the deal, saying it is concerned only with a comprehensive cessation of aggression and a full Israeli withdrawal. Lebanon's president Joseph Aoun called the strikes "a flagrant violation of Lebanese sovereignty." Both sides are due to meet again the week of the 22nd of June, but the prospects look dim.

Peru goes to the polls today in a tight presidential runoff between right-wing candidate Keiko Fujimori and leftist Roberto Sanchez. Fujimori, the daughter of the late authoritarian president Alberto Fujimori, is running on a tough-on-crime agenda after a surge in extortions and murders. Sanchez is pledging a 33 per cent increase in the minimum wage and greater state control of natural resources. This is Fujimori's fourth attempt at the presidency, and polls suggest a polarised but very close race.

The Strait of Hormuz remains at a virtual standstill despite the Iran ceasefire now being in its third month. Daily ship traffic has fallen from a prewar average of more than 130 vessels to just nine, as companies refuse to transit without clearer security guarantees. Iran insists ships need its permission and has suggested imposing a fee for passage. Pope Leo the Fourteenth, meanwhile, said on his way to Spain that the Iran war does not qualify as a "just war" under Catholic teaching.
