The lead story is a major blow to Keir Starmer's government. Defence secretary John Healey resigned last night in a blistering letter accusing the prime minister and Treasury of failing to commit the resources the country needs at "this dangerous time." Healey had sought an additional 18 billion pounds for defence over four years, but Rachel Reeves would go no higher than around 13 billion, bringing spending to just 2.68 per cent of GDP. He said the settlement falls well short of what is required. Hours later, armed forces minister Al Carns followed him out the door. Dan Jarvis, the former paratrooper and security minister, has been appointed as the new defence secretary. The double resignation was the lead story on last night's BBC News at Ten. Healey is the sixth minister to leave the government in the past month, and this one cuts particularly deep given the active military commitments in the Strait of Hormuz, the NATO Arctic mission, and the backdrop of the Iran conflict and intensifying Russian threats.

On AI, the one new development since yesterday's briefing is Google Gemini 3.5 Live Translate, which rolled out on Monday and Tuesday. It is a speech-to-speech translation model built into the Google Translate app on Android and iOS, covering more than 70 languages. Unlike older turn-by-turn systems, it translates continuously, staying just a few seconds behind the speaker while preserving intonation and vocal pitch. It is also rolling out to Google Meet for business customers. A genuinely practical tool if you are travelling or interviewing in other languages.
