Googlebook will launch on premium devices, but more-affordable options are coming

Google is creating a new class of device called the Googlebook, launching this autumn, and the first wave will be deliberately expensive. Chrome Unboxed reports that these debut machines are positioned as super premium, with rigid manufacturing specifications, the company's signature Glow Bar, and top-tier silicon from Intel, Qualcomm and MediaTek paired with high-performance neural processing units for on-device AI. The logic is that Google wants to set a quality baseline and properly showcase its Gemini features before worrying about price.

The strategic point is that this echoes how Chromebooks established themselves, and Google is explicit that affordability is coming later rather than at launch. VP John Maletis put it plainly: Google has "always been about enabling technology and the ability to be productive and access information regardless of your price point. And so over time we will come down, but these first devices are super premium."

On timing, the piece suggests that within one to two years, as processor technology matures, manufacturers should be able to build mid-range Googlebooks in the $400–$600 bracket, using more efficient chips such as Snapdragon X Plus or mid-tier MediaTek parts, while keeping the core Googlebook identity and a consistent layout. The eventual audience is the familiar one for cheaper computing — students, casual users and budget-conscious buyers — who for now are being asked to wait while the premium tier does the establishing work. The takeaway: a top-down launch, premium first and mainstream later, a notable inversion of how some rivals seed a new category.

MediaTek confirms Dimensity chips for new Googlebooks

The second piece, also from Chrome Unboxed, drills into the silicon and spots a meaningful branding shift from MediaTek. The chipmaker has historically kept its flagship Dimensity name for smartphones while using its Kompanio line for Chromebooks, including devices like the Lenovo Chromebook Plus 14 and the Acer Chromebook Plus Spin 514. For Googlebooks, though, MediaTek is bringing the premium Dimensity brand to laptops for the first time, under a new tier it calls Dimensity CX — CX standing for "Compute Experience."

MediaTek's reasoning is that it "makes a lot of sense," because the Googlebook OS builds on Android technology, and MediaTek already powers billions of Android devices worldwide. The claimed technical benefit is that this shared Android DNA allows native optimisation and removes software translation layers, which should mean better performance, hardware-level AI processing, and smoother transitions between an Android phone and a Googlebook.

As with the first article, the emphasis is heavily on local AI, with powerful NPUs enabling fast, energy-efficient on-device experiences, and Gemini Intelligence running with what MediaTek describes as "immediate responsiveness directly on the device" while protecting battery life for all-day use. The broader signal is about positioning: by reaching for its top smartphone brand rather than its laptop brand, MediaTek is telling the market it sees Googlebooks as a genuinely new, premium product category, worthy of flagship treatment rather than the more workmanlike slot Chromebooks have occupied.
