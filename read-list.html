Anthropic is quietly turning Claude into a productivity OS and it’s awesome

By Parth Shah, XDA Developers, 1 August 2026. A first-person account rather than a reported piece. Shah’s argument is that Claude has stopped being a chatbot in the way he uses it, and has become the central layer his working life runs through. He opens by describing the old pattern — start a blank chat, ask a question, copy the answer out, go somewhere else to do the actual work. That is no longer how it works: “At this point, calling Claude a chatbot feels inaccurate.”

He builds the case around three features. The first is Projects, which he treats as dedicated workspaces rather than a filing system for old chats. He keeps separate spaces for article ideas, Android research, AI tools, personal productivity, coding experiments and business documents, each carrying its own instructions, reference material and uploaded files, so the assistant already knows the context when he comes back. Over time this has turned into something closer to a personal knowledge management system, with standing spaces for topics he returns to regularly and useful reports or PDFs dropped in as he finds them. He will sometimes get Claude to convert messy material into a clean Markdown document and save that back into the same knowledge base.

The second is Cowork, which he frames as the shift from advisor to operator. Rather than being told how to organise his files, he grants access to a folder, states the outcome he wants, and lets the tool do the classification work. His running example is a Downloads folder clogged with screenshots, invoices, installers and documents with meaningless filenames, which gets inspected, grouped, restructured and renamed based on contents. He is careful to say he still reviews the proposed changes and does not hand over unrestricted access to sensitive folders, but the point stands that he is now delegating the productivity workflow itself rather than asking for advice about it.

The third is connectors, linking Claude to the apps he already depends on: tasks in Asana, documents in Google Drive, mail in Gmail, meetings in Google Calendar, designs in Canva, interface concepts in Figma. The example he gives is asking it to check the calendar for an upcoming meeting, find the relevant email thread, pull supporting material from Drive and help him prepare a brief, without him first assembling any of it by hand.

His conclusion is deliberately modest about the displacement claim. Claude doesn’t replace an operating system or any dedicated productivity app, he writes, “but it is becoming the layer that connects them all.” He signs off by noting he hasn’t even touched on Claude Code, which he calls arguably the best tool in its segment, and which he says deserves an entirely separate discussion.

Worth flagging that this is enthusiast commentary from a tech writer, not analysis with independent evidence behind it, and the piece carries no counterweight beyond his own caution about sensitive folders. The reader comments underneath supply some. One consultant prefers keeping project context in versioned files in a repository, on the grounds that knowledge held inside the tool can drift from what is actually in the code while a file in the repository cannot. Another makes the point that most companies and organisations will not allow an external app that interacts with the file system and the underlying operating system, and will probably block installing it in the first place — which makes the whole setup easier for freelancers than for anyone inside a large employer.
