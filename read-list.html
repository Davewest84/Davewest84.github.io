I started using Gemini Personal Intelligence on Android, and it's been a productivity booster

Writing in Android Police, Parth Shah describes how Google's Gemini Personal Intelligence has changed the way he uses his Android phone. The feature, which sits inside the Gemini app's settings, has two main parts. The first is called Memory, which lets Gemini learn and remember facts about him that recur across conversations. He mentions telling it once that he's a vegetarian, that he prefers bulleted summaries, and that he's working on a residential project in Vesu, and finding it carries that context across into later prompts without having to be reminded. The second is called Instructions, which lets him define how Gemini should communicate — e.g. break long paragraphs into bullet points, use simpler language, or act as a technology analyst looking for edge cases. He says the combination removes the constant repetition of background that wastes time at the start of every query.

Beyond preferences, Personal Intelligence deepens the integration between Gemini and the wider Google ecosystem (Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Keep, Tasks, Photos, Docs, YouTube Music) plus connected third-party apps including WhatsApp, Spotify and GitHub. Shah gives several practical examples of multi-step queries that pull information from across these apps: fetching the latest credit card bill from Gmail in seconds; retrieving a specific photo of a tech setup from Google Photos; or, more elaborately, asking Gemini to find a pizza recipe in Google Docs and create a grocery list in Google Tasks based on it, or to combine flight details from Gmail with a packing list from Keep and an itinerary document in Drive into a single trip summary.

Shah's verdict is that Gemini Personal Intelligence has moved Gemini "from a novelty to a necessity", with the strength being less the chat answers and more the ability to handle real tasks across connected apps. The piece reads as a strong endorsement rather than a critical assessment.
