The budget: No rabbits, but a prevention PDEL and good growth for health (perhaps?) (David Buck, LinkedIn)

Buck's starting point is that this budget was never going to produce a rabbit out of the hat. But he picks out one genuine surprise: the commitment to a prevention departmental expenditure limit, or PDEL. The idea, campaigned for by Demos, the Health Foundation, the Institute for Government and others, is to put prevention spending on a footing similar to capital spending in the government's accounts.

He concedes it sounds dry, but argues it matters for transparency. In his words, "if we can't see what government is actually spending or committing to prevention how can we really know whether there is more or less of it, or government is walking the walk not just talking the talk?" He adds a big caveat: it only works if consistent definitions of prevention can be agreed, which he calls a big task in itself. He notes the new Welsh government has made similar commitments in principle, and that Scotland and English health systems are also doing more to measure prevention.

He then lists other budget measures with health effects beyond the NHS: levies on gambling, tax cuts for small alcohol producers, more money to cut rough sleeping, and help with energy costs.

His bigger argument is about the fiscal devolution framework published alongside the budget. It gives mayoral authorities more funds and loans to deliver "good growth". Buck wants that to mean growth which reduces health inequalities, in line with the new duties placed on mayors by the Community Empowerment and Devolution Act. He finds it "curious" that the framework does not mention health or prevention once, even though the prime minister referred to avoidable health inequalities at PMQs just before the budget speech.

He closes by calling this the first of three acts, with the prime minister's plan for government and the rewiring of the state to come, and asks for more join-up between the Treasury, DHSC and Number 10.

One reply is worth noting. Sir Chris Ham commented: "Surprising the degree of uncritical excitement about PDEL especially when we have no idea yet how it will work."
