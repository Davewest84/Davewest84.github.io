Why AI paper tablets are becoming serious productivity tools (TechRadar)

Note: this is sponsored content — an advertorial created with tablet maker iFLYTEK — rather than an independent review. Treat it as a sales pitch, not editorial.

The argument is that a new breed of E-ink tablet — the kind that looks and feels like writing on paper — has quietly become a real productivity device now that AI has been built in. The pitch leans on the familiar complaint that our phones, once liberating, have become constant sources of distraction. The proposed answer is a device that helps you focus, delivers information when you need it, lasts all day on battery, and does not cause eye strain, because an E-ink screen reflects ambient light like paper rather than shining light at you — which also makes it far easier to read in bright sunshine.

What lifts these beyond simple e-readers is the software. The piece claims today's models can take voice instructions, transcribe meetings while identifying who is speaking, turn handwriting into searchable text, scan printed documents, and query multiple large AI models. The running example is iFLYTEK's own AINOTE 2, which it says holds a Guinness World Record as the world's thinnest E-ink tablet at 4.2mm, with a 10.65-inch, 300dpi display, up to 14 days of battery life, handwriting-to-text in 133 languages, and Android 14 with full Google Play support. It says the meeting transcription can use GPT-5 to pull out action points and key takeaways. A smaller model, the AINOTE Air 2, adds a camera for scanning, a front light and a speaker.

The most useful idea here, once you cut through the marketing, is the framing that these devices let you be a contributor in a meeting rather than a recorder — the tablet captures and summarises while you simply take part. The rest is a discount offer (20% off both models) that has already expired.
