Gemini Intelligence has high spec requirements on Android

Google's new Gemini Intelligence platform sets a high hardware bar: a flagship-class processor, at least 12GB RAM, AI Core, Nano v3, plus five OS upgrades and six years of quarterly security patches.

The Nano v3 requirement is the decisive constraint. Devices as recent as the Pixel 9 series and Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 7 are still on Nano v2, so they're locked out, along with the OnePlus 13 and Oppo Find N5.

Qualifying phones are almost all 2026 releases — Pixel 10 line, Galaxy S26 line, OnePlus 15/15R, Oppo Find X8/X9, Honor Magic 8 Pro, plus models from Motorola, iQOO, Realme and Vivo. Launch later in 2026, likely on Galaxy Z Fold 8 first.

The wider story: Google using premium AI features to drive hardware upgrades, the way Apple has done with Apple Intelligence.

I tested AI tools on data analysis — here's how they did

Paul Bradshaw, who teaches data journalism, set ChatGPT (GPT-4o and GPT-5), Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Flash and Copilot (GPT-4-turbo) a 10,000-row UK gender pay gap dataset.

The core finding: "AI tools can perform accurately on calculations they make, but those aren't always the right calculations to answer your question." All four could generate and run code; Claude was best at handling ambiguous prompts.

Failures: without checking the code, ChatGPT produced plausible-looking wrong answers. Vague prompts like "biggest pay gap" caused tools to miss negative values (companies where women out-earn men). Only Claude flagged tied results. Classification prompts with false premises exposed a "gullibility bias". Asked to find Birmingham companies, every tool searched the address text rather than the postcode — except Claude, which alone tried postcode validation.

Bradshaw's checklist: verify code is actually being run; name exact columns and methods; define ambiguous terms up front; ask for ranked lists; invite the model to flag flaws. The responsibility for accuracy stays with the analyst.

Interview with Hannah White — Folk and Tumble

South London Americana singer-songwriter Hannah White on her sweep at the UK Americana Music Awards — three trophies including Artist of the Year and Album of the Year for Sweet Revolution. Her husband and musical partner Keiron Marshall took Instrumentalist of the Year.

Her forthcoming album In Human Nature is "a bit more mature" and in places darker; Keiron calls it "more cinematic". Sweet Revolution nods to her song Clementine, using fruit as a feminist metaphor: "Being a woman in a man's world, is an act of revolution."

The interview covers her unusual background — working-class South London, a father active in the BNP, mental health struggles, periods of homelessness while raising her son. Music as therapy, but also perspective: "seeing yourself as a separate small part of something bigger, it lightens the load."

Alongside the music, she and Keiron run The Sound Lounge, a 14-year-old South London venue that's part gig space, part community service, providing free events, meals and warmth. On political songwriting: "You can't separate politics from life." Her song Car Crash addresses homelessness and shoplifting, written to humanise the people behind the stereotypes.
