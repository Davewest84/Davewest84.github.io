Birmingham & Solihull PCNs — three locality tests compared

The same 175 GP practices in 36 PCNs (32 multi-practice), shown three times. Each panel rings the practices belonging to PCNs that fail a different test. The progression from top to bottom is from the loosest definition of incoherence to the strictest. B&S is England’s worst-performing ICB on each measure.

In a flagged PCN — thick red ring on yellow fill. The practice is a member of a PCN that fails the panel’s test.
In a passing PCN — thin grey ring. Coherent on this measure.

1. Delaunay non-contiguous — 24 of 32 multi-practice B&S PCNs (75%)

138 of 175 practices ringed
Members are not direct Voronoi neighbours of each other. The original test — flags any PCN whose territory is technically broken at the practice level, including minor boundary cases.

2. MSOA-disconnected — 15 of 32 multi-practice B&S PCNs (47%)

81 of 175 practices ringed
Members live in MSOA neighbourhood blobs that are not connected to each other. Stricter than Delaunay non-contig — forgives same-locality fragments that touch via foreign practices.

3. Intermingled — 14 of 32 multi-practice B&S PCNs (44%)

83 of 175 practices ringed
Convex hull contains 2+ foreign-PCN practices AND members’ 5-NN homophily is under 40%. The strictest test — only flags PCNs whose territory is genuinely shared with several other PCNs and whose members live mostly among non-colleagues.

Source: NHS Digital ODS, April 2026. PCN colours are consistent across the three panels (e.g. SmartCare Central PCN keeps the same colour throughout).
