Intermingled PCNs

A stricter test than non-contiguity. A PCN is “intermingled” if its convex hull contains 2+ foreign-PCN practices AND its members’ average 5-nearest-neighbour homophily is under 40%. Nationally, 166 of 1,210 multi-practice PCNs (13.7%) meet both criteria. 16 of those are technically contiguous — the original Delaunay test missed them.

Intermingled — thick red ring on a yellow fill. The PCN’s territory contains members of two or more other PCNs and its members live mostly among non-colleagues.
Non-contiguous (boundary case) — thin orange ring. Flagged by the Delaunay test but doesn’t meet the higher bar; mostly minor edge effects.
Contiguous — thin grey ring. Members form a coherent geographic block.
