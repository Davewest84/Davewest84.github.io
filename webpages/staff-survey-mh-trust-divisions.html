Notes on the data

Source: NHS Staff Survey breakdown reports 2023, 2024 and 2025 (cms.nhsstaffsurveys.com), Breakdown 1. Divisions are defined by each trust. They are matched across years by name, ignoring cost-centre codes, level tags and differences of spelling or abbreviation (each such match checked by hand); a division that was renamed with a change of scope, or restructured, appears as separate rows. A division-year with fewer than 50 responses on the measure shown is not plotted. Where a matched division's response count changed more than twofold between years, it may have been restructured under the same name: its change is marked † and not flagged.

The trust score is the unweighted "Your org" figure in the breakdown report; region and mental health averages are the simple mean of those trust figures, so they differ slightly from the weighted averages in the benchmark reports. Region and sector averages include only trusts reported in all three years, so their change is like for like. The 2023 safe-and-healthy figures are the corrected, republished ones.

Division types are assigned by HSJ from each division's name, so they are approximate. A unit that joins two service lines with no lead one (for example learning disability and forensic) is treated as mixed. The mental health average for a type is the simple mean of divisions of that type, across the 48 trusts, that had 50 or more responses in all three years and no more than a twofold change in size (a consistent cohort, so the trend is not an artefact of which divisions were reported when). Where fewer than ten such divisions exist, the average, marked "2025 only", covers every division of that type with 50 or more responses in 2025. Types not comparable across trusts have no average: place-based localities, whole-trust "clinical operations" and unsplit adult mental health units, units combining two service lines, staff-group rows and the one trust reported as a single unit. These hold about two-fifths of 2025 responses, so the type averages describe trusts organised by service line.

Change is flagged where it is 0.2 or more as displayed, an editorial threshold rather than a statistical test. Dot size follows that year's response count. The published breakdowns give response counts but not the spread of answers, so no confidence interval can be calculated for a division; treat small dots, and changes between them, with more caution.
